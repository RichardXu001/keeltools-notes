Note 05 · Career

Career shifts in the AI era: turn “uses AI” into verifiable skill

2026-10-20 · ~7 min

“Proficient with AI tools” on a resume means nothing — interviewers have seen the line a thousand times, and one follow-up (“which one, for what?”) exposes it.

The short version: the evidence chain has three links — the tools you use, the task you used them on, and a checkable result. With all three, “uses AI” stops being an adjective and becomes a fact.

01 Why the phrase fails

Skepticism about AI claims is earned: many AI-titled roles ask for something else entirely, and many AI line items collapse under a second question. The fix isn't stronger wording — it's more specific wording: tool name + task + a number. Drop one of the three and the sentence reverts to an adjective.

02 What the demand side looks like

“AI jobs” split three ways: for engineers (ship models into products), for operators (use models inside processes), and a hybrid that appeared in recent years — Palantir's forward-deployed engineer is the template: sitting at the customer site, translating model capability into a concrete deliverable. All three want evidence, and none accepts vagueness.

For full breakdowns — capability layers and a checklist for reading JDs — the main site has a long-form piece: The FDE stack: five layers, job requirements and a screening checklist; role-by-role summaries live on the roles page.

03 How to write the evidence

The same experience, twice
❌ Vague✅ Evidence
Proficient with AI for productivityCut first-round PR feedback from 2 days to 4 hours using Claude plus a self-built review checklist
Familiar with LLM applicationsTurned the product catalog into an MCP server so support can query it from any chat client — 3 minutes saved per lookup
Follows AI trendsWeekly digest of competitors' AI features; supported 3 pricing decisions over 12 weeks

What the right column shares: a tool name, a task boundary, and a number or decision consequence. Interviewers can drill in — and drilling in is exactly the ground you prepared.

04 The action list

Four things doable in two weeks

List the AI tools you actually use and write down which task each one serves — anything you can't answer gets deleted. Pick one repetitive job and automate it with a platform or a skill, recording the time saved. Turn those two into evidence sentences for your resume and intro. Then check yourself line by line against a target JD: how many lines can your evidence answer? The gaps are your next two weeks of study.

Where to find the tools

The main-site directory is sorted into tools, skills and prompts — the skills and prompts sections are meant to be borrowed from: an entry like Code Review Assistant is a ready-made starting point for evidence sentence two. For a feel of what “wiring several AIs together” looks like in practice, VaneTools wrote up Pi-cowboy, orchestrating Codex, Claude Code and OpenCode as subagents — projects like that are resume evidence themselves.

If you keep one rule: write AI experience as tool + task + result, because a hiring manager only asks one follow-up — and you must be able to carry it.
