Meeting notes tools: live transcription and document Q&A are different jobs

2026-10-20 · ~6 min

Under the “AI meeting notes” label sit two products: ones that transcribe while the meeting runs, and ones that answer questions over your materials afterwards. Compare them on a single feature table and you will pick the wrong one.

The short version: Otter.ai wins during the meeting; NotebookLM wins after it. Most teams end up with both, but transcription comes first — you cannot question material that was never captured.

01 What each product actually does

Otter.ai is live transcription: it joins your meeting audio, produces text in real time, segments by speaker, and leaves you a searchable record. NotebookLM is document Q&A: you don't feed it a live meeting, you feed it a pile of material — notes, docs, transcripts — and ask questions in natural language, with answers carrying citations.

One line to remember the split: Otter answers “what was said in the meeting”; NotebookLM answers “what these documents say”.

03 Two typical setups

Sales or customer calls

You can't take notes while the call runs, and afterwards you need “what budget did the client mention last time?” That is Otter's home turf: transcription, speakers and search all live on the meeting's timeline.

Research and review work

A dozen interviews, meeting notes and background docs, and you need to cross-ask “which conversations mentioned the pricing change, and what was the basis each time?” That is NotebookLM's home turf: feed the material in, get cited answers you can verify line by line.

04 The decision line

Ask one question: does the pain happen during the meeting or after it? During — transcription. After — Q&A. Both? Start with transcription: unaskable questions come from uncaptured material.

Full profiles, pricing and pros/cons on the main site: Otter.ai's page and NotebookLM's page; the main-site blog also has a six-dimension head-to-head: Otter.ai vs NotebookLM, decided task by task.

If you keep one rule: don't ask which is stronger — ask whether your pain lives during the meeting or after it.
