A new site's first month of SEO: checking the index beats publishing

2026-10-23 · ~6 min

A new site has no search data in its first month, so the worst use of that month is writing more articles. The first real job is a single one: make sure search engines can find, crawl and understand every page you want to rank.

The short version: month one is three webmaster consoles, one sitemap submission, and a weekly indexing review — plumbing first, content cadence second.

01 Three consoles, none optional

Every search engine runs its own webmaster console and none shares data with the others: Google Search Console for Google (verify the property, submit the sitemap in the Sitemaps report, use URL Inspection for per-page status); Bing Webmaster Tools for Bing and Copilot (it can import straight from Search Console, halving the setup); add Baidu's platform if Chinese-speaking users matter. Skip this and every later “why isn't it indexed?” question is unanswerable — no data, no diagnosis.

02 The three files every site needs

Minimum config before launch
FileJobCommon mistake
sitemap.xmlTells engines every indexable pageNew pages missing; noindex pages included
robots.txtDeclares crawl rules and points to the sitemapNo Sitemap line — or the file 404s entirely
Canonical tagsEach URL points at itself; no duplicate-content splitBilingual sites missing reciprocal hreflang

One easy-to-miss trap: on static hosting, a 404 doesn't raise an alarm — it just silently shrinks your index. Curl every URL in the sitemap before submitting; we self-checked this GitHub Pages notes site exactly that way and found the missing robots.txt on the first pass.

03 Review weekly, not daily

There is no “indexed immediately” after submitting: on a fresh domain, Google's first crawl usually takes days to weeks. Review weekly: week one checks that the sitemap was accepted and error-free; week two inspects pages one by one for indexed/not-indexed; week four watches impressions move off zero. Four consecutive weeks with zero pages indexed is when you go back to technical causes — robots blocks, canonical errors, content quality — not when you rewrite everything after week one.

04 Content follows the plumbing

Key pages first, long tail second

Homepage, directory, core product pages — get these indexed first; they are the skeleton of how the site appears in search. Long-tail articles come after the skeleton is stable, or you keep rearranging structure while publishing and dilute the signals.

Internal links are the second pipe

Indexed is not ranked: a new page should be linked from at least one already-indexed page on the day it goes live. The first link graph doesn't need to be complex — home → key pages → new article is three hops, enough for a crawler to walk.

For a checklist you can run top to bottom, the KeelTools blog has a full-length piece: the first-month SEO checklist for a new site; keyword research pairs with it in keyword research with Google Trends, and the link side in which backlinks are worth doing and which are risky.

If you keep one rule: month one's KPI is pages indexed, not articles published. Content without plumbing just spins in place.
